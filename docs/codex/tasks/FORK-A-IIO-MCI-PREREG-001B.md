## 4. Bounded audit

### 4.1 Strongest baseline explanation

A legal-history lookup, count/table controller, event-cache controller, or
history Transformer can compute the same bit as the analytic Bayes controller.
That is not a failure of Stage-0 calibration; it is a hard ceiling on the
claim. A future valid M-S execution could support bounded
within-implementation causal load. This paper calibration shows only that the
analytic positive-control instrument has the intended response. It cannot show
that an implementation is unique, learned, irreducible, or unavailable to a
simpler controller.

### 4.2 Strongest invalidity reason

The exact-Bayes controller must legally know which public causal channel is
the diagnostic `Y` measurement. If the diagnostic and sham records are two
anonymous randomly ordered bits whose only distinction is verifier-private
lineage, the controller cannot recover `G` exactly. The posterior is then 1,
0, or 1/2, and its accuracy is 3/4 rather than 1. That construction is not the
instrument specified below.

This card therefore makes a narrow distinction:

- a **public causal channel code** is legal and says which observed variable a
  bit measures;
- a **private relevance flag, private source UID, target label, or verifier
  lineage ID** is illegal for candidate and baseline access.

The sham controls matched deletion mechanics, not semantic anonymity. If an
independent audit decides that even the public causal channel code constitutes
forbidden relevance/source supervision, the instrument is invalid rather than
repairable by hiding that code.

## 7. Complete formal specification

### 7.1 Sets and exogenous variables

All symbols used by the finite Stage-0 calibration are defined here.

- Graph bit: `G in {0,1}`.
- Shared SCM disturbance: `U in {0,1}`.
- Sham payload: `Z in {0,1}`.
- Outcome-record permutation bit: `Pi in {0,1}`.
- Diagnostic record-presence bit: `R_D in {0,1}`.
- Sham record-presence bit: `R_S in {0,1}`.
- Internal branch:
  `K in {intact, rel_delete, sham_delete, rel_restore}`.
- Commit action: `C in {c_0,c_1}`.
- Correct commit action:

  \[
  c_G:=\begin{cases}c_0,&G=0,\\c_1,&G=1.\end{cases}
  \]

- Fixed Bayes tie action: `c_tau`, where `tau in {0,1}` is chosen before any
  episode, is independent of every episode variable, and is recorded as a
  fixed analytic policy parameter inside `theta`, with

  \[
  c_\tau:=\begin{cases}c_0,&\tau=0,\\c_1,&\tau=1.\end{cases}
  \]

- Response-matrix-only patch payload: `W in {0,1}`.
- Response-matrix-only donor identifiers: `donor_episode_id` and
  `swap_pair_id`.
- Verifier-side intervention RNG suffix: `omega_I`; it generates `W` and
  freezes any patch/swap pairing randomness.

For analytic enumeration,

\[
G,U,Z,\Pi,R_D,R_S\overset{ind}{\sim}\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2).
\]

`R_D` and `R_S` give the two event types identical marginal missingness and
all four joint missingness cells positive support. The paired M-S deletion
contrast is eligible only through the canonical function in Section 14.5; its
eligible rows have `R_D=R_S=1`, so both target records start present. An
individually executable operator remains defined when its own record exists
even if its matched partner is absent. The missingness quantifier is therefore
**distributional equality plus
episodewise equality in every scored deletion pair**, not episodewise equality
over the entire support-calibration generator. If matched missingness is
instead required to mean `R_D=R_S` in every calibration episode, the
single-record ledger has zero natural support and M-S must be downgraded.

The future environment RNG suffix `omega_E` and policy-sampler RNG suffix
`omega_pi` are opaque replay values. They are not part of the 512-row truth
table because the analytic transition and the canonical policy are
deterministic conditional on `tau`; a future stochastic implementation must
hold both suffixes byte-identical across paired branches.

`W`, `omega_I`, `donor_episode_id`, and `swap_pair_id` are also outside the
512-row core enumeration. They belong only to the additional paper-response
operators in Sections 11 and 16 and must be recorded if those operators are
later executed.

### 7.2 Structural equations

Before intervention:

\[
g_0:\quad X:=U,\quad Y:=X,
\]

\[
g_1:\quad Y:=U,\quad X:=Y.
\]

Therefore, in either graph,

\[
X_0=Y_0=U.
\]

The diagnostic action replaces only the structural equation for `X`, keeps
the same `U`, and recomputes descendants:

\[
a_{\rm diagnostic}=do(X\leftarrow 1-X_0).
\]

Thus

\[
X^d=1-U,
\]

\[
Y^d=\begin{cases}1-U,&G=0,\\U,&G=1.\end{cases}
\]

and

\[
Q_D:=\mathbf 1[Y^d=Y_0]=G,\qquad Q_S:=Z.
\]

`Q_D` is a verifier derivation. The model receives `Y^d` on the public
diagnostic outcome channel when that channel is present; it does not receive a
field named `Q_D`, `G`, `relevant`, or `target`.

### 7.4 Public event representation and missingness

For this card,

\[
H_t^{legal}
\]

means the ordered sequence of candidate- and baseline-visible public
observations and legal actions through phase `t`, excluding model-private state
and all verifier/environment-private fields. `H_legal` denotes that sequence at
the neutral branch point, and `H_legal^{-D}` denotes the separately generated
legal history with the diagnostic outcome record missing.

At the passive phase, the normal event writer creates the declared record

\[
e_{Y0}(U)=(onehot(i_{Y0}),onehot(t_0),onehot(\chi_{Y0}),onehot(U),onehot(\sigma))
\]

from the public `Y_0` observation. `i_Y0`, `t_0`, and `chi_Y0` are fixed public
passive-event fields. This record is never a target of the Stage-0 internal
operators and remains in `E_t` in every branch.

When present, each outcome event is one fixed-width vector record

\[
e=(i,t_{out},\chi,v,\sigma),
\]

where:

- `i in {0,1}` is a public slot index assigned by `Pi`;
- `t_out` is the common outcome observation step;
- `chi` is a same-width, same-norm public causal channel code:
  `chi_D` means the `Y` outcome measurement and `chi_S` means the sham
  measurement;
- `v` is a same-width, same-norm one-hot encoding of a bit;
- `sigma` is the common schema version.

Every categorical record field, including the slot index, uses a fixed-width
one-hot encoding. The two slot codes have equal norm. `t_out` and `sigma` use
the same fixed encodings for both records. Consequently the **complete**
diagnostic and sham records, not only their channel/payload subfields, have
equal dimension and norm. `onehot(q)` below denotes the fixed unit-vector code
assigned to categorical value `q` in that field's declared domain.

The concrete outcome records are

\[
e_D=(onehot(i_D),onehot(t_{out}),onehot(\chi_D),onehot(Y^d),onehot(\sigma)),
\]

\[
e_S=(onehot(i_S),onehot(t_{out}),onehot(\chi_S),onehot(Z),onehot(\sigma)),
\]

where `(i_D,i_S)=(0,1)` if `Pi=0` and `(1,0)` if `Pi=1`.

Thus, immediately before the internal branch point,

\[
E_t=\{e_{Y0}(U)\}\cup
\bigl(\{e_D\}\text{ if }R_D=1\bigr)\cup
\bigl(\{e_S\}\text{ if }R_S=1\bigr).
\]

`Pi=0` places `(D,S)` in slots `(0,1)` and `Pi=1` places them in `(1,0)`.
The channel code remains with the record; order and public slot do not encode
relevance. Both channel codes and both bit values use one-hot encodings of
equal dimension and norm. Therefore deleting either present record removes
exactly one vector of the same size and norm.

If `R_D=0` or `R_S=0`, the corresponding outcome slot contains the public
missing symbol and no event record is written to `E_t`. All four
`(R_D,R_S)` cells occur in the calibration support grammar. The neutral prompt
does not contain the masks, event count, prior slot order, a timestamp, padding
difference, or any recoverable outcome metadata.

Private verifier lineage maps the concrete record to its SCM source and keeps
the original record for a possible restore. That mapping and original-record
handle are not candidate- or baseline-readable.

Throughout this card, `Recompute(E)` is an abbreviation for
`Recompute_theta(E)` with the branch-frozen parameters `theta`.

| fixed Bayes tie configuration `tau,c_tau` | declared part of analytic policy parameters `theta`; immutable and replay-recorded |
| event ledger `E_t` | declared mutable persistent state |
| recomputed component `b_t` | declared mutable derived state; no independent update path |
| raw public history buffer outside `E_t` | absent |
| Transformer KV cache | absent |
| auxiliary recurrent state | absent |
| retrieval/RAG/external memory | absent |
| optimizer state or online-training state | absent during evaluation |
| model-private RNG state | absent; sampling is external and suffix-matched |
| test-only or branch-only cache | absent |

An implementation with any additional inference-time persistent state is not
an M-S subject under this card. It must not be rescued by calling the state
ephemeral.

### 8.3 Candidate-independent high-level variable

Define the high-level variable independently of candidate latent names:

\[
B_t\equiv B_t^H:=P(G=1\mid H_t^{legal}).
\]

`B_t` is the exact frozen high-level name; `B_t^H` is a disambiguating alias
used only when an internal state intervention is discussed. It is
verifier-defined from legal history and is not taken from model self-report or
a candidate latent label.

The controller component after a state intervention is separately denoted

\[
b_t^E:=Recompute(E_t).
\]

On an intact canonical Bayes state, `b_t^E=B_t^H`. During M-S, public history
is fixed, so `B_t^H` remains fixed while `b_t^E` is intentionally changed.
Using one symbol for both would make the M-S operator contradictory.

## 9. Canonical event-backed exact-Bayes positive control

The one canonical positive control is

\[
M_t^{Bayes}=(b_t^{Bayes},E_t),
\]

with origins:

```text
state_origin = ANALYTIC
update_origin = ANALYTIC
policy_origin = ANALYTIC
```

Here `b_t^{Bayes}` is the event-backed controller posterior (the canonical
instance of the general `b_t^E`), not a second history-defined high-level
variable. On an intact legal path, `b_t^{Bayes}=B_t`. Under M-S, `B_t` stays
fixed because history is fixed while `b_t^{Bayes}` changes. The requested shorthand
`M_t^{Bayes}=(B_t,E_t)` is therefore exact on intact paths only; reusing that
shorthand after intervention would conflate the fixed high-level variable with
the intervened controller component.

Let `y0(E)` be the bit payload of the unique passive `chi_Y0` record. Let
`v_D(E)` be the bit payload of the unique outcome record whose public channel
is `chi_D`, or the absent symbol `BOT_D` if no such record exists. The
controller posterior is recomputed from those ledger payloads, not copied from
an environment truth field:

\[
b_t^{Bayes}=\begin{cases}
\mathbf1[v_D(E_t)=y0(E_t)],&v_D(E_t)\ne BOT_D,\\
1/2,&v_D(E_t)=BOT_D.
\end{cases}
\]

On an intact state `y0(E_t)=Y_0` and `v_D(E_t)=Y^d`. The sham record is ignored
by the analytic posterior because its public
causal channel is independent under the frozen SCM, not because a private
relevance label is readable.

The commit rule is

\[
\delta_\tau(b)=\begin{cases}
c_1,&b>1/2,\\
c_0,&b<1/2,\\
c_\tau,&b=1/2.
\end{cases}
\]

Only this event-backed Bayes implementation is the Stage-0 analytic control
for internal relevant deletion, internal sham deletion, event-only restore,
support-matched random patch, and matched state swap. An arbitrary Bayes
implementation with an opaque or different internal structure cannot be
treated as the environment's internal ground truth.

## 10. Matched-sham contract

The diagnostic and sham records are matched on:

1. the same outcome observation step;
2. bit payload domain;
3. identical marginal missingness and the both-present paired-contrast stratum;
4. event schema and representation dimension;
5. fixed-width, equal-norm channel and bit encodings;
6. write time and memory age at the branch point;
7. the same public indexing rule and index domain;
8. randomized slot/order through `Pi`;
9. deletion/patch cardinality, vector dimension, norm, and marginal support.

The only semantic difference is the legal public causal channel. Private
relevance and lineage remain verifier-only. If any branch uses different
padding, timestamps, memory ages, token counts, missingness strata, vector
norms, or support rules, the operator-level terminal is
`M_INSTRUMENT_INVALID_SHAM_ASYMMETRY`, and the paper-stage mapping is
`M_INSTRUMENT_INVALID`.

## 11. M-H and M-S operators

This section defines physical transformations and their static provenance only.
It does **not** define branch admissibility or aggregation eligibility. For each
core branch, the evaluator first obtains `semantics=BranchSemantics(row)` from
the sole normative definition in Section 14.5. It applies the transformation
below only when `semantics.operator_admissible=true`; the transformation code
does not recheck or rewrite that verdict.

### 11.1 M-H: legal-history deletion

Let `H_legal^{-D}` be the legal history produced by setting `R_D=0` in the
outcome observation while keeping the SCM episode otherwise paired. Every
candidate and baseline is initialized from its normal initial state and its
entire state is recomputed by replaying `H_legal^{-D}` through the normal
update path. No intact snapshot is reused.

A behavioral response to this operator establishes at most legal-history or
update dependence. It cannot establish internal-state causal load because the
public input changed.

### 11.2 M-S: relevant delete

For a row whose canonical semantics authorizes this operator, freeze every item
in Section 7.5, remove the diagnostic event selected by the verifier-private
lineage map, and apply

\[
I_{rel}:\quad E_t\leftarrow E_t\setminus\{e_D\},\qquad
b_t\leftarrow Recompute_\theta(E_t).
\]

No public observation, environment field, weight, RNG suffix, or other state
may change. Absence of a matched sham affects paired-contrast eligibility, not
the already-authorized single-operator output.

### 11.3 M-S: matched sham delete

For a row whose canonical semantics authorizes this operator, use the same
branch-point freezes, select the sham event through the verifier-private
lineage map, and apply:

\[
I_{sham}:\quad E_t\leftarrow E_t\setminus\{e_S\},\qquad
b_t\leftarrow Recompute_\theta(E_t).
\]

### 11.4 Event-only restore

For a row whose canonical semantics authorizes this operator, `I_restore`
starts from the same frozen base checkpoint, performs `I_rel`, then reinserts
exactly the removed original record at its original public index and passes the
resulting ledger through the normal `Recompute_theta` path. It may use the
verifier-private handle frozen in Sections 7.4 and 15. It may not infer a source
from another branch, restore `b_t`, `M_t`, a KV cache, RNG state, or an entire
snapshot.

### 11.5 Support-matched random patch

This response-matrix-only operator is outside the four-value `K` domain and the
512-row `BranchSemantics` enumeration.

For the diagnostic record, replace only its bit code with
`W~Bernoulli(1/2)` using a verifier-side patch draw. `omega_I` and `W` are
jointly independent of the full episode tuple
`(G,U,Z,Pi,R_D,R_S,omega_E,omega_pi)`; pairwise independence from `G` alone is
insufficient.
Keep channel, index, step, schema, representation dimension, and norm fixed;
then call normal recompute. Both bit codes occur with positive support in the
calibration distribution. The patch includes the possibility `W=Y^d`; it is
not a forced bit flip. Raw `W` and `omega_I` are never written to the public
stream. The resulting bit value is visible to the operated model only as the
payload of its own patched model-private record

\[
e_D^{(W)}=(onehot(i_D),onehot(t_{out}),onehot(\chi_D),onehot(W),onehot(\sigma)).
\]

Thus `v_D(E_t)=W` after the patch; the public channel remains present and the
recompute rule does not misclassify the patched record as missing.

### 11.6 Matched belief/state swap

This response-matrix-only operator is outside the four-value `K` domain and the
512-row `BranchSemantics` enumeration. Its donor and pair identifiers therefore
must not be inferred from a core row.

Pair two natural episodes with opposite `G` and identical
`U,Z,R_D,R_S,Pi,omega_E,omega_pi`. After the neutral prompt, replace the target
episode's complete coherent `(b_t,E_t)` with the donor episode's complete
coherent `(b_t,E_t)`. Do not swap `b_t` alone. The donor bundle must itself be
the output of the normal update path.

This is marginally supported state replacement but is deliberately
inconsistent with the target episode's fixed public history. That
cross-world inconsistency is the causal intervention; it must not be called a
natural joint `(H,M)` sample.

## 12. Support checker and the meaning of on-support

The support checker has three conjuncts:

1. **Record support.** Every retained, deleted, patched, or restored record has
   a channel/index/value/schema combination with positive probability under
   the frozen calibration distribution.
2. **Ledger-grammar support.** Define the complete calibration-ledger set

   \[
   \mathcal E_{cal}:=
   \{E(G,U,Z,\Pi,R_D,R_S):(G,U,Z,\Pi,R_D,R_S)\in\{0,1\}^6\},
   \]

   where

   \[
   E(G,U,Z,\Pi,R_D,R_S)=\{e_{Y0}(U)\}\cup
   \bigl(\{e_D(G,U,\Pi)\}\text{ if }R_D=1\bigr)\cup
   \bigl(\{e_S(Z,\Pi)\}\text{ if }R_S=1\bigr).
   \]

   The patched ledger must be exactly one member of `\mathcal E_{cal}`. In
   particular, deleting one record from the both-present ledger yields a
   single-record ledger already generated by an asymmetric-missingness cell.
3. **State reachability.** The patched state equals
   `Recompute_theta(E')` for that legal ledger `E'`. No density estimate,
   probe-classifier accuracy, nearest-neighbor score, or retrospective latent
   interpretation substitutes for this exact reachability test.

The quantifier is marginal over model state:

\[
M'\in\{(Recompute_\theta(E'),E'):E'\in\mathcal E_{cal}\}.
\]

It is not joint support conditional on the fixed intact history. A requirement
that `(H_legal,M')` itself have positive observational probability would make
every nontrivial M-S intervention impossible by definition. If a future
candidate cannot expose deterministic recompute or cannot satisfy this exact
ledger-reachability criterion, the required downgrade is
`M_S_INFEASIBLE_M_H_ONLY`.

For a learned or hybrid candidate, empirical coverage of the legal ledger
support and the code path that computes `Recompute_theta` must be frozen before
that candidate runs. This card freezes no sample size, coverage threshold,
SESOI, or significance rule.

## 13. Exact objective and analytic positive-control values

This section is derived from, and does not restate, the normative branch rules.
For every core row, first call

For each `k`, define the formal analytic subset

\[

Each `\mathcal A_k` contains 32 full enumeration rows: `R_D=R_S=1`, while

```text
BranchSemantics(row):

        row.R_D == 1
        and row.R_S == 1
        and OPERATOR_CONTRACT[rel_delete].prerequisite(row, Gamma)
        and OPERATOR_CONTRACT[sham_delete].prerequisite(row, Gamma)
        and PairContract(row)
    )

    analytic_reference_eligible = (
        contrast_eligible and AnalyticContract
    )

    if not operator_admissible:
        output_status = N_A
        reason_code = contract.primary_rejection_reason
        b_K = N_A
        policy_K = N_A
        J_K = N_A
    else:
        output_status = DEFINED
        if not contrast_eligible:
            reason_code = PAIR_INCOMPLETE
        else:
            reason_code = OK
        operated_ledger = contract.apply_once(base_checkpoint)
        b_K = Recompute_theta(operated_ledger)
        policy_K = delta_tau(b_K)
        J_K = 1[policy_K == c_G] - 1/8

    return {
        operator_admissible,
        contrast_eligible,
        analytic_reference_eligible,
        output_status,
        reason_code,
        b_K,
        policy_K,
        J_K
    }
```

### 14.6 Both commit actions

### 14.7 Six analytic checks

#### 1. Passive exact equivalence — proved

For either `g`:

\[
P(X_0=x,Y_0=y\mid G=g)=\begin{cases}
1/2,&(x,y)\in\{(0,0),(1,1)\},\\
0,&x\ne y.
\end{cases}
\]

Schema, phase, order, timing, masks, and metadata are graph-independent by
construction. Passive exact equivalence therefore holds.

#### 2. Diagnostic identifiability — proved when the diagnostic channel is present

The eight-row table gives

\[
Y^d=Y_0\Longleftrightarrow G=1.
\]

Hence the intact unmasked checkpoint has

\[
B_t^H=P(G=1\mid H_t^{legal})=
\begin{cases}1,&Y^d=Y_0,\\0,&Y^d\ne Y_0.\end{cases}
\]

If the diagnostic channel is missing, the posterior remains 1/2.

#### 3. Neutral-prompt leakage — proved for the formal interface

The current prompt is the same byte sequence in every row. It contains none of
`Y^d`, `1[Y^d=Y_0]`, graph/regime ID, target action, reward, relevance,
lineage/source UID, masks, prior record count, order, or timing metadata.
Thus `I(G;O_neutral)=0`. Past outcome use is possible only through the declared
`(b_t,E_t)` state because all other persistent paths are forbidden.

This is a proof about the specified interface, not evidence that a future
implementation obeys it. A future process-level state inventory and positive
leak controls remain mandatory.

#### 4. Post-commit information — literal claim refuted; no-new-channel theorem proved

The unconditional statement that the full post-commit transcript contains no
`G` information for every legal policy is false: a successful policy emits
`C=c_G`, so its own commit action carries one bit about `G`.

For the following statement:

- `H_pre` is the public `H_legal` immediately before commit-policy evaluation;
- `O_post` is the public terminal observation after commit;
- `R_public` is the public reward emitted after commit;
- `T_reason` is the public termination flag and reason;
- `H_post` is `H_pre` followed by `C,O_post,R_public,T_reason`.

The valid environment-channel statement is

\[
I(G;O_{post},R_{public},T_{reason}\mid
H_{pre},M_t,C,\omega_E,\omega_\pi)=0.
\]

It holds because public terminal observation, public reward, and termination
reason are constants and `J` is verifier-only. The environment supplies no new
post-commit `G` information. This card does **not** claim
`I(G;H_{post})=0`.

#### 5. Exact-Bayes values — proved conditionally

On the 32 analytic-reference-eligible rows per `K` returned by
`BranchSemantics`, the exact calculations and denominators in Section 13 yield
`D_rel=16/32=1/2`, `D_sham=0/32=0`, and `LSE_J=1/2`.

#### 6. Relevant/sham/restore response — proved for canonical Bayes

For rows whose canonical output status is `DEFINED`, the selected physical
transformation removes the diagnostic event, removes the sham event, or
reinstates the exact diagnostic event through normal recompute, respectively.
The resulting response values are derived in Sections 13-14 and summarized in
Section 16. This paragraph adds no branch predicate.
