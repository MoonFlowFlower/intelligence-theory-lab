## Redesign Principle

Future Gate4 must specify evidence targets before task mechanics.

The target is not "make another dynamic partner-belief POMDP." The target is:

```text
Can a candidate maintain and update hidden partner-relevant state under uncertainty, use it for counterfactual partner prediction and action under distribution shift, and degrade under real interventions in ways that faithful shortcut baselines cannot match?
```

## Anti-Hardcoding Audit

Hard-coding risk:

- Future tasks must not reward hand-authored partner types, scripted social behaviors, prompt-level roles, finite answer tables, or schema fields that encode the target.
- Any explicit partner latent field visible to the candidate or baseline must be treated as leakage unless the candidate and baselines are both being tested for a declared decoder-access upper bound.

Local optimum risk:

- A single narrow toy family can be optimized without a reusable mechanism.
- Future success must require heldout transfer across partner families, task schemas, and counterfactual dynamics.

Zeno-trap risk:

- 002C already triggered closure. Another small patch would consume effort without changing the target.
- Future design must include closure conditions that stop after baseline tie rather than invite incremental hardening.

Evidence leakage risk:

- Future traces must scan serialized state, file names, config fields, trace IDs, episode order, context IDs, action names, prompt text, fixture names, and scoring artifacts.
- Leakage scanners must include positive-control cases.

Weak-baseline risk:

- Baselines must be independent callable implementations with access-rights declarations and provenance.
- Strawman variants of the candidate do not count as faithful baselines.

Schema-split risk:

- Train, heldout, and counterfactual splits must not differ in schema, field presence, ID format, file path, or artifact naming in a way that exposes the answer.

Second-logic-path risk:

- Candidate scoring, replay, ablation, and baseline scoring must share one declared evidence path.
- No alternate scoring path may pass without invoking the claimed computation.

Replay weakness:

- Future replay must recompute candidate and baseline behavior from serialized state plus observation.
- Stored actions, cached outputs, and hash comparison alone are invalid.

## Baseline-Collapse Lessons From 002C/002D

The 002C candidate produced a perfect score, but the evidence target was still recoverable by faithful non-mechanism routes. 002D correctly treated the ablation drop as residual diagnostic value only. It showed candidate path dependence, not mechanism advantage.

Therefore the redesign requirement is not "add stronger ablations" alone. It is:

- require independent baselines first;
- require transfer where static table and trace matching fail;
- require counterfactual interventions that are not recoverable from visible fields;
- require replay recomputation from serialized state plus observation;
- require leakage positive controls;
- define negative-evidence stop conditions that close the route on any faithful baseline tie.

## Candidate Task-Family Options

Option 1: cross-family social causal transfer.

- Mechanism target: infer partner-relevant latent causal structure from partial observations and update it under counterfactual interventions.
- Expected baseline failure mode: lookup and n-gram baselines fail on unseen causal families; serialized-state and full-bundle decoders fail if state contains only candidate-computable sufficient statistics, not answer encodings.
- Candidate advantage condition: candidate beats strongest faithful baseline on heldout partner families and counterfactual interventions with complete provenance.
- Why this is not 002C patched: the target is cross-family causal transfer, not same-family route recovery.

Option 2: active uncertainty-reducing partner probing.

- Mechanism target: choose probes under budget to reduce uncertainty about partner dynamics before acting.
- Expected baseline failure mode: static belief tables and pair counts fail when the useful query depends on current posterior uncertainty and unseen partner family.
- Candidate advantage condition: candidate's query choices improve heldout and counterfactual prediction beyond query-capable imitation and trace retrieval baselines.
- Why this is not 002C patched: the task tests information acquisition policy under uncertainty, not passive decoding of partner observation streams.

Option 3: distribution-shifted belief revision with delayed consequences.

- Mechanism target: revise partner belief after partial evidence and maintain it across delayed outcome windows.
- Expected baseline failure mode: frequency and nearest-neighbor baselines collapse under shifted observation/outcome mappings and heldout schemas.
- Candidate advantage condition: candidate maintains calibrated belief updates and degrades under memory-freeze and observation-order scramble ablations.
- Why this is not 002C patched: the evidence target is delayed belief revision under distribution shift, not immediate route selection.

Option 4: social-memory counterfactual replay.

- Mechanism target: reconstruct future partner response from auditable prior interaction state while resisting pure retrieval.
- Expected baseline failure mode: nearest-neighbor trace retrieval and generative-agent style memory summaries fail when counterfactual branches require recomputation rather than recall.
- Candidate advantage condition: replay recomputation and counterfactual perturbation results change as predicted under real interventions, while trace-only replay and memory-summary baselines fail.
- Why this is not 002C patched: the focus is replayed social-state update under counterfactual perturbation, not partner-type table selection.
